Kadyrov brands Chechens who left for Europe “despicable traitors”

Chechnya’s leader, Ramzan Kadyrov, has launched a fresh verbal attack on the diaspora, describing Chechens who fled to Europe and criticise the republic’s authorities as “pseudo-patriots” who have cut themselves off from their roots. He said they are unwanted at home and that there is no way back for them.

In his telling, these exiles live on foreign money, spread rumours about their own people and undermine the peace that, he says, thousands of Chechen men died to secure. He added that Chechen soil has no place for anyone who betrays the memory of those he calls heroes.

The message was posted on his Telegram channel alongside a video of him picking pomegranates, strolling through Grozny with associates and singing a Chechen song. He did not say what prompted it. Critics and rights groups have long said that relatives of Kadyrov’s opponents abroad face pressure inside Chechnya.

Shootout confirmed as opposition claims unrest

Kadyrov said one person suspected of sabotage was killed and another detained, after a meeting with law-enforcement chiefs. Interior Minister Aslan Iraskhanov told him that police work had identified people linked to “acts of sabotage committed in the past”.

Kadyrov gave no date, place or method, and said nothing about security-force casualties. The opposition group NIYSO alleges that some officers were killed. OC Media notes it is the first armed incident officially confirmed in Chechnya in more than a year.

At the same meeting Kadyrov argued that if a man takes up arms and kills an officer, his whole family should bear responsibility – a stance rights groups have repeatedly condemned as collective punishment.

Fifth term begins on Kadyrov’s 50th birthday

Kadyrov was sworn in for a fifth term on Monday 5 October, the day he turned 50, promising in his speech to respect residents’ rights. He has run the republic since 2007.

Official results from the 18–20 September vote gave him 99.85% (about 830,000 votes) on a reported turnout of 95.19% – higher than his previous record. Observers outside the official system treat such numbers with deep scepticism.

OC Media pairs the ceremony with the human-rights record: Human Rights Watch’s 2026 report says authorities kept targeting relatives of his opponents in 2025, while the Memorial centre describes a new wave of raids, abductions and forced contract-signing with the Defence Ministry.

Another group of Chechen “volunteers” sent to Ukraine

On 30 October Kadyrov announced on Telegram that a new contingent of what he described as volunteers was being dispatched to the war in Ukraine. Chechen authorities announce such departures regularly.

Critics doubt that everyone going is doing so willingly. Memorial’s 2026 reporting describes raids, abductions and pressure to sign military contracts, which is the backdrop to those doubts. The official line, as always, is that recruits are enthusiastic.

SocietyJul 2026 · Novaya Gazeta Europe

Half of divorced couples “reunited” – often under pressure

State TV reports that since a commission was created in July 2017, it has run 25,124 inspections and found 6,020 divorced couples. After its intervention 3,038 couples got back together, 2,344 declined, and 905 could not reconcile for religious reasons.

The independent outlet Caucasian Knot reports that most of these reunions happen under duress, and residents commenting online say reconciliations are “voluntary-compulsory”.

The campaign follows a spike in divorces: official figures show 9,070 in 2022, with Chechnya’s rate of 437 per 100,000 in 2024 well above neighbouring Ingushetia, Kabardino-Balkaria and Karachay-Cherkessia.

Health rumours and the succession question

Exiled media reported earlier this year that Kadyrov was seriously ill; Ukrainian outlets cited kidney failure. These claims are unverified. Chechen officials denied that he was hospitalised after he missed a State Council meeting, and a video of him was published in early January.

Speculation about a successor focuses on his sons. Adam Kadyrov, secretary of the republic’s Security Council, oversees the police, received the title Hero of Chechnya for a second time in June and appeared in lieutenant’s uniform at a public event in September.

In February Kadyrov dismissed reports of a serious car crash involving Adam as “fake news”, saying AI can fool anyone.
